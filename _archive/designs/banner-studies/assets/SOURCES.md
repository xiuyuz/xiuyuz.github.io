# Anatomical surfaces

The three anatomical banner studies use a real MRI-derived cortical mesh, not a procedurally approximated brain. Their materials, contours, hemisphere spacing, and presentation are authored for this design gallery. They are illustrations, not diagnostic views.

## Source and license

- Dataset: OpenNeuro **ds006128**, “Data for ‘Modeling 2D Spatio-Tactile Population Receptive Fields of the Fingertip in Human Primary Somatosensory Cortex’”, subject `sub-01`.
- Pinned snapshot: **1.0.11**; DOI: <https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds006128.v1.0.11>.
- Dataset repository: <https://github.com/OpenNeuroDatasets/ds006128>.
- Dataset license: **CC0 1.0 Universal**, <https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/>.
- Intermediate mesh: `public/models/brain-atlas.glb` from <https://github.com/StarKnightt/brain-explorer>, obtained together with its `brain-atlas.provenance.json` and `NOTICES.md`.
- Intermediate GLB SHA-256: `ce761741d866e32d7a5b7638f4cacd5ec03e2b582e72c8218aba483ac28125e8` (10,692,408 bytes).
- Its provenance identifies the dataset snapshot tag commit as `20faeed0c774665f58d55c409992f32c8dc4b76a`.

The intermediate atlas uses the subject's bilateral FreeSurfer `lh.pial.T1` and `rh.pial.T1` cortical surfaces, matching white surfaces, morphometry, cortical annotations, and `aseg.mgz` segmentation. The upstream generator simplifies the cortical surface while retaining its topology and smooth normals, derives internal structure meshes from segmentation, and reorients the data to Three.js coordinates. Upstream provenance and third-party notices are retained in `brain-atlas.provenance.json` and `brain-atlas-NOTICES.md` beside this file.

## Local adaptations

`cortex.bin` contains the intermediate atlas's `unified-cortex` mesh: **75,132 vertices / 150,256 triangles**. Positions were translated to the bounding-box center and uniformly scaled to a longest dimension of 2.7. The original surface normals and indexed topology are preserved. Axes are X = right, Y = up, Z = anterior. Exact bounds, normalization, byte counts, and file SHA-256 values are in `cortex.metadata.json`.

`cortex-contours.bin` contains **75,914 line segments** generated by intersecting that cortex with 37 evenly spaced horizontal planes, from Y = −0.85 in 0.047-unit steps. Interpolated surface normals accompany both segment endpoints. Endpoints are lifted 0.0025 units along the surface normal to avoid overlap with the faint underlying shell. Study 02 gives these line segments a narrow ribbon width and physically lit silver material at runtime. The contours represent geometric cross sections of the surface, not neural pathways.

`cortex-inner.bin` contains the **corpus callosum, bilateral hippocampi, and bilateral amygdalae** from the same intermediate GLB, with the same translation and scale as the cortical surface. It retains the upstream geometry and normals. See `cortex-inner.metadata.json` for structure offsets, counts, normalization, and the file SHA-256 value. Study 03 separates cortical hemispheres for visibility and applies translucent materials; its color and hemisphere spacing are artistic choices.

## Binary formats

All scalars use little-endian byte order.

- A surface record starts with two `uint32` values: vertex count and index count. These are followed by `vertexCount × 3` `float32` positions, `vertexCount × 3` `float32` normals, and `indexCount` `uint32` triangle indices.
- `cortex.bin` consists of one surface record.
- `cortex-inner.bin` concatenates three surface records. Their names and starting byte offsets are in `cortex-inner.metadata.json`.
- `cortex-contours.bin` has no header. Every segment occupies twelve `float32` values: endpoint A position XYZ, endpoint A normal XYZ, endpoint B position XYZ, endpoint B normal XYZ.

The procedural surfaces in the other gallery studies are authored in the scene modules and do not use external anatomy data. The locally bundled Three.js code has separate license information in `../../vendor/three/SOURCES.md`.
